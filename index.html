About

I am an incoming DPhil candidate in Sociology at the University of Oxford. My research focuses on migration, integration, and inequality, with a particular emphasis on how institutional conditions shape life-course trajectories.

I apply causal inference methods and use large-scale administrative data. I am especially interested in how early legal status and institutional uncertainty affect migrants’ long-term outcomes.

Research

Legal Certainty and Early Integration: Causal Evidence from a Migration Shock in Iceland

Using population-wide administrative data and quasi-experimental methods, my DPhil project estimates the causal effects of early institutional conditions on employment, welfare dependence, and longer-term integration trajectories.

Publications

  • Skills and Labour Market Integration of Immigrants and their Children in Iceland (2024), OECD Publishing.
  • An integrated approach to service delivery for people with multiple and complex needs (2024), OECD Working Papers.

Background

I have worked across academic and policy settings, including the OECD, EFTA, and the University of Iceland. My work has focused on migration policy, labour market integration, and applied quantitative research.

I hold a Master’s degree in European Affairs (cum laude) from Sciences Po and a BA in Philosophy from the University of Iceland.
